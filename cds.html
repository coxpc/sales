Context

Why Dealers Are Asking

The CDK breach in June 2024 shut down thousands of dealerships for weeks. Dealers aren't just curious about data security — they're scared. This is no longer a compliance checkbox. It's a business continuity issue.

⚠

The CDK Moment Changed Everything

In June 2024, a ransomware attack on CDK Global forced 15,000+ dealers off their DMS for weeks. Service advisors wrote ROs by hand. F&I managers couldn't fund deals. Some dealers lost hundreds of thousands of dollars per day. The incident became the single most referenced concern in dealer conversations about platform selection — and it wasn't a Cox system. But dealers started asking every vendor the same question: "How do I know you're different?"

⚖

FTC Safeguards Rule: Dealers Are Now Accountable Too

The updated FTC Safeguards Rule (effective June 2023) requires auto dealers to implement a comprehensive information security program. Dealers must encrypt customer PII in transit and at rest, implement multi-factor authentication, designate a qualified security officer, and vet their third-party service providers. Dealers can be fined and held liable if their vendors aren't compliant. That means your products are part of their audit.

🔍

What Cox's Own Competitive Intelligence Found

Cox Automotive's CI team benchmarked data security communications across 7 competitors in July 2024. The finding: most vendors publish vague, abstract statements. Tekion leads the industry with a centralized Trust Portal. CDK has moderate detail spread across multiple pages. Reynolds & Reynolds and DealerSocket (Solera) publish almost nothing specific. Cox has an opportunity to lead — but the story has to be told. That's what this guide is for.

Compliance Landscape

Frameworks That Apply to Your Dealers

These are the regulations dealers reference when they ask about data security. Know them. Connect them to Cox capabilities in every conversation.

GLBA

Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act

Federal law requiring financial institutions — including auto dealers — to safeguard customer financial information. The FTC Safeguards Rule is GLBA's enforcement mechanism. Dealers must have written security programs and vendor oversight.

FTC

Safeguards Rule (Updated 2023)

Mandates encryption in transit and at rest, MFA for all systems with customer NPI, annual penetration testing, access controls, and a designated qualified individual overseeing the program. Cox products must demonstrate compliance to pass dealer audits.

CCPA/CPRA

California Consumer Privacy Act

Grants California residents the right to know what data is collected, to delete it, and to opt out of its sale. Cox has a dedicated SOP for Consumer Privacy Request Processing (v1.0, May 2026) covering all Retail Solutions products.

SOC 2

Service Organization Controls

Third-party audit standard covering security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. SOC 2 Type II reports cover a period of time (vs. a point-in-time snapshot) and are the gold standard dealers and lenders request during vendor evaluations.

PCI DSS

Payment Card Industry Standard

Applies wherever payment card data is processed or stored — directly relevant to Dealertrack F&I and DMS payment processing. Ensures cardholder data environments are segmented and protected to prevent financial fraud.

VCDPA

Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act

Virginia's data privacy law (effective Jan 2023) with consumer rights similar to CCPA. Cox's Enterprise Privacy Standard (v1.0, April 2026) governs how all Cox Automotive products handle state-level privacy requirements across all 50 states.

Sales Enablement

Dealer Objection Handlers

The questions dealers actually ask — and how to answer them without reading from a spec sheet.

Are Cox products SOC 2 certified? Tekion told us they are.
Tekion has made SOC 2 a marketing centerpiece — and honestly, good for them. What we can tell you is that Cox's security program is built to SOC 2-equivalent controls — encryption, access management, monitoring, availability, and confidentiality. The enterprise security program runs annual penetration testing, SAST/DAST scanning, and a formal incident response process. The difference is that we don't lead with the badge — we lead with the architecture. If you need formal third-party audit documentation for an insurance or lender requirement, let's get you connected with our security team to provide the right documentation.
What would happen to our data if Cox got hit like CDK?
Fair question — and you should ask it of every vendor. The CDK incident was a ransomware attack that succeeded because of specific architectural vulnerabilities in their infrastructure. Cox runs its products across AWS, Azure, and OCI cloud environments — not a single legacy data center model. We have a formal Business Continuity program, annual penetration testing, and a dedicated Enterprise Risk & Security team. The honest answer is no system is immune, but our architecture, our detection capabilities, and our incident response process are specifically designed to limit the blast radius and recover faster than CDK did.
We just got audited for FTC Safeguards. What documentation can Cox provide?
Cox supports dealer FTC Safeguards compliance in a few direct ways. MFA is available across VinSolutions, Dealertrack, and Dealer.com — check the box on authenticated access. Encryption is in place for data in transit and at rest across all Retail Solutions products. Data access controls let you demonstrate role-based access to NPI. And if your auditor needs a formal vendor security assessment or data processing agreement, Cox's legal and security teams can provide documentation. Ask your PC or AM to open that request.
We had a former employee who we think pulled customer data when they left. Can you help?
This is exactly why audit logging and access controls matter. VinSolutions and Dealertrack maintain activity logs showing who accessed which records and when. If a former employee exported customer data or accessed records they shouldn't have, there's a log trail. The first thing you should do is have your admin revoke all access and change shared credentials immediately — then review the activity log. If you suspect a serious breach, Cox has an incident response process, and depending on the scope, you may have legal notification obligations under your state's data breach law.
Our GM wants a one-pager on what Cox does to protect our customer data.
Here's the short version you can share right now: Cox Automotive encrypts all customer data in transit and at rest. Access to customer records requires authentication and is controlled by role. Cox has a formal Enterprise Privacy program with a dedicated Privacy Office. All Retail Solutions products are covered under Cox's Enterprise Security Testing program including annual penetration testing. Cox has formal processes for consumer data deletion and privacy requests under CCPA, VCDPA, and related laws. And all data-sharing partners — OEMs, lenders, third parties — must meet Cox's third-party security standards via formal agreements.
Is customer data from our VinSolutions CRM used to train AI or shared with other dealers?
Dealer customer data is not shared with other dealers — full stop. Your CRM records are specific to your rooftop and are not visible to competing dealers. Regarding AI: Cox's AI development is governed by a formal policy, and any use of data for AI model training would be disclosed in your contract terms and governed by Cox's privacy standards. If this is a contractual concern, your account team can walk you through the specific data use terms in your agreement. This is a legitimate question to ask — and the answer should be in writing.
