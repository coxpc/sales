⚖ Regulatory Frameworks
GLBA
Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act
Federal law requiring financial institutions — including auto dealers — to safeguard customer financial information. Cox products are built to meet GLBA's data protection and written security program requirements.
FTC Safeguards
Updated Rule · June 2023
Mandates encryption in transit and at rest, MFA for all systems with customer NPI, annual penetration testing, access controls, and vendor oversight. Dealers can be fined if their vendors aren't compliant. Cox products satisfy every requirement.
CCPA / CPRA
California Consumer Privacy Act
Grants California residents rights to know what data is collected, to delete it, and to opt out of sale. Cox has a dedicated Consumer Privacy Request SOP (CAI-SOP-P005, May 2026) covering all Retail Solutions products.
SOC 2
Service Organization Controls
Third-party audit standard covering security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy. SOC 2 Type II covers a period of time — the gold standard requested by lenders and large dealer groups during vendor evaluations.
PCI DSS
Payment Card Industry Standard
Applies wherever payment card data is processed — directly relevant to Dealertrack F&I and DMS payment processing. Ensures cardholder data environments are segmented and protected to prevent financial fraud.
VCDPA +
State Privacy Law Coverage
Cox's Enterprise Privacy Standard (April 2026) governs compliance across all 50 states. A dedicated Privacy Office monitors emerging state legislation — dealers aren't left to figure out each new law on their own.

💬 Dealer Objection Handlers
Are Cox products SOC 2 certified? Tekion told us they are.
Tekion has made SOC 2 a marketing centerpiece. Cox's security program is built to SOC 2-equivalent controls — encryption, access management, monitoring, availability, and confidentiality. We run annual pen testing and SAST/DAST on every release. If your lender or insurer needs formal third-party audit documentation, your AM can connect you with our Enterprise Risk & Security team directly.
What would happen to our data if Cox got hit like CDK?
The CDK incident succeeded because of specific vulnerabilities in a legacy data center model. Cox runs across AWS, Azure, OCI, and GCP — not a single legacy infrastructure. We have a formal Business Continuity program, annual penetration testing, and a dedicated Enterprise Risk & Security team. No system is immune, but our architecture and incident response are specifically designed to limit blast radius and recover faster than CDK did.
We just got audited for FTC Safeguards. What can Cox provide?
MFA is available across VinSolutions, Dealertrack, and Dealer.com. Encryption is in place in transit and at rest. Role-based access controls let you demonstrate NPI governance to auditors. If your auditor needs a formal vendor security assessment or data processing agreement, Cox's legal and security teams can provide documentation — ask your AM to open that request.
A former employee may have pulled customer data when they left.
This is exactly why audit logging and access controls exist. VinSolutions and Dealertrack log who accessed which records and when. First step: revoke all access and change shared credentials immediately, then review the activity log. If you suspect a breach, Cox has a formal incident response process — and depending on scope, you may have state breach notification obligations your attorney should review.
Is our customer data used to train AI or shared with competing dealers?
Dealer customer data is not shared with other dealers — full stop. Your CRM records are specific to your rooftop. Regarding AI: Cox's AI development is governed by a formal policy and any use of data for model training is disclosed in your contract terms. If this is a contractual concern, your account team can walk you through the specific data use terms in your agreement — the answer should be in writing.
Our GM wants a one-pager on what Cox does to protect our data.
Short version to share right now: All customer data encrypted in transit and at rest. Role-based access controls across all products. Formal Enterprise Privacy Office with 50-state coverage. Annual penetration testing across all production environments. Consumer privacy request process under CCPA, VCDPA, and related laws. All OEM, lender, and third-party data partners required to meet Cox's security standards via formal Data Safeguarding Addenda.
